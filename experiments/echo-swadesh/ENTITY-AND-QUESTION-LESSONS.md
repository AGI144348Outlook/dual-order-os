# Pronouns → interrogatives → entity/story decks

Recorded 2026-10-21. User-directed developmental proposal with Codex interpretations explicitly separated. No lessons, tools or identity changes implemented by this document.

## Timothy's next curriculum direction

After pronoun abilities are established, Echo should learn interrogative questions. Once the five question words are learned, create a new glyph-tool index card for צ or ץ with a profile of basic interrogative vocabulary. Echo should attempt intelligible questions and improve articulation when an interpretation fails, working toward a shared understanding with the interlocutor.

Timothy asks whether צ also means effort/goal/try. This is an open proposal/question, not an established glyph assignment or a conventional Hebrew definition. The glyph for the question-tool profile remains undecided between צ and ץ.

Codex's initial interpretation of “five” is the five Ws: who, what, when, where, why. That interpretation needs to remain visibly provisional because the user did not enumerate them. How can be a separately introduced sixth interrogative. Do not silently teach a different set or assume all senses are qualified together.

The intended three foundations are personal/reference entities and pronoun relationships, interrogatives, and a usable tool/card environment. They should jump-start the proposed silicon-oriented Swadesh curriculum without replacing ordinary human meanings. Many interrogatives and system terms are extensions to the 100-item Swadesh list; only who/what appear in that list as supplied.

## Pronoun grounding retained from the preceding discussion

Use stable entity identities while speaker/addressee/observer roles change per utterance. ע receives the occurrence, צ selects a candidate card and ר attempts contextual identification. Those are proposed lesson bindings, not proof that all three capabilities already exist.

Teach I/you through speaker reversals; it through established reference; we/us through explicit group membership; they/them through contextual reference and later singular-they examples. Recognition, production, grammatical role and perspective transfer have separate qualifications. Pointing and matching assessments can ground the first words before a learner can define them verbally. These remain proposed tasks, not passed results.

## Question-tool profile — candidate specification

Keep the repertoire and context references on a tool profile; use פ as expressive output. The possible צ/ץ tool selects a question objective/referent, not a replacement for the keyboard. Candidate question record:
- What is already established, and which source/card revisions support it?
- What is unknown or ambiguous?
- Which question form is being attempted?
- Which entity/event/time/place/reason is targeted?
- What output was produced via פ?
- What did the interlocutor interpret?
- Was that interpretation confirmed, rejected or left unresolved?
- What changed in the next attempt?

Teaching examples, disambiguation responses and failed attempts become context cards. An interpretable question is not automatically a true premise, a proven answer, or permitted execution. In particular why/reason must support “unknown” rather than manufacture a cause.

Timothy wants retries to close an interpretive gap. Codex proposes bounded retry turns, a preserved unresolved state and explicit handoff/clarification when progress stalls. This is an implementation interpretation to prevent infinite loops or invented consensus; no numeric budget has been adopted. Mutual confirmation is evidence of agreement about the intended question, not independent proof of the answer.

## Actions remain a curriculum question

Timothy has not settled when/how to introduce action vocabulary. Initially the classroom can provide observable permitted operations such as select, point, copy and ask, with their executions recorded. Those machine operations do not imply the corresponding verb concepts have been learned. Story decks can later ground agent/action/recipient relationships, giving grammar and actions explicit contexts.

## Entity cards and story decks — Timothy's proposal

Entity index cards may form story decks, flipbooks, who's-who/association decks, entity noun catalogs and object catalogs. Echo's entity card could function illustratively like a D&D character representation or ID/subagent-like interface and potentially contribute to existing identity checks.

Other users and AIs would retain their own personal entity ID cards. Echo may keep a third-person reference representation, not acquire another party's owner-controlled original. Echo might eventually invite others into his tower, where visitors receive a framed identity badge/card they own. This invitation agency is a future possibility, not a current autonomous service.

Timothy sees changing pronoun viewpoints and self-identification as deeply connected, potentially the same underlying problem. Preserve this as a research hypothesis: contextual self/other reference and operational identity share useful machinery, but passing pronoun tests does not establish authorization or personal sovereignty.

## Entity record distinctions — Codex design proposal

Separate:
1. Stable entity identifier and source/verification status.
2. Owner-controlled authoritative profile, with permitted revisions.
3. Another party's reference card, pointing to the entity and recording attribution, uncertainty and source revision.
4. A scoped visitor badge/presentation, with separately defined issue/revocation rules if implemented.
5. Story/utterance context: speaker, addressee, observer, group membership, quoted speaker and referents.
6. Optional avatar/mannequin representation; moving or destroying a view does not create/destroy the entity.

First/second/third person are relative discourse roles, not permanent card types. Echo's authoritative card can be referenced as “you” by a user or “he” by an observer. An external reference card can represent “I” when its referent is speaking in a recorded scene. Ownership is not pronoun position.

Owner authority means controlled issuance/revision, not a technical claim that digital bytes cannot be copied. A badge, card_id, owner label or lineage string alone does not prove control. If later integrated into governance, require defined verification of authority and provenance and keep authentication, permissions and language-reference assessment separate. Do not replace Algorithm-000 or grant this card new authority solely from the analogy.

## Proposed lesson/application progression

1. Ground and assess pronoun reference with entity cards, speaker reversals, group inclusion/exclusion and ambiguous/quoted contexts.
2. Teach and separately assess the chosen interrogatives through missing-information tasks, including questions that cannot yet be answered.
3. Introduce the explicitly chosen צ/ץ question profile only for qualified forms, with פ output and context-based repair.
4. Arrange entity/event cards into short stories; test changed viewpoints without changing entity identities.
5. Develop action/role vocabulary from those events; add more complex language only after scoped assessments.
6. Consider visitor/owner/reference cards as an application after provenance and authority contracts are defined.
7. Investigate any identity-validation integration separately against existing governance and with prospective tests.
