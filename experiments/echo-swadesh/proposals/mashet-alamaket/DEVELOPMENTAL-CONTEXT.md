## Codex's proposed developmental distinctions

The Swadesh 100 numbering is not a human acquisition order. Human learning involves overlapping exposure/attention, recurrence recognition, perceptual grouping, reference and labeling, differentiation, intentional action, composition/reasoning, and monitoring/correction. Exact acquisition varies by language, environment and task; a produced word is not proof of generalized comprehension.

A proposed Echo curriculum separates:
1. Encounter and continuity: preserve source, input, order and attribution.
2. Recognition and differentiation: distinguish repetition, difference and unfamiliarity.
3. Grounded vocabulary: link candidate senses with examples, counterexamples and context.
4. Composition: preserve entities, participants and relationships while combining expressions.
5. Inference/correction: separate observation, assumptions, conclusions and uncertainty.
6. Learning regulation: select clarification, practice, consolidation and transfer tasks.
7. Bounded agency: select authorized operations, evaluate outcomes, stop safely.
8. Accountable autonomy: maintain resource/decision histories and pass independently scoped evaluations.

This is an engineered curriculum proposal, not established biological equivalence or a proof of consciousness or sovereignty.

## Status distinctions for encounter cards

- Seen: an identified source occurrence is recorded.
- Previously encountered pattern: another occurrence resembles earlier material.
- Identified: evidence supports an entity/category assignment, with alternatives retained.
- Named: a designation is associated with the referent or candidate.
- Qualified for use: specific usage tests support a scoped ability.

A second encounter can support “something resembling this has appeared before” without justifying “I know what this is”. Green requires relevant sense/use qualification and Matrix presence; familiarity alone is insufficient. Recording a Seen stamp is not jurisdictional approval.
